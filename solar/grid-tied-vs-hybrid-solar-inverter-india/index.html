Grid-Tied vs Hybrid Solar Inverter in India: Which Should You Choose?

A rooftop solar inverter is not just a box that converts DC to AC. Its operating mode determines whether your system can export energy, charge a battery, and supply selected loads during a grid outage. Choose the system architecture around the job you need it to do.

Quick decision

Choose a grid-tied inverter when your main goal is reducing grid electricity purchases and you do not need solar-powered backup during an outage.

Consider a hybrid inverter when you want solar, grid and battery integration with backup for selected circuits, and you are prepared to size and maintain a battery system.

Neither choice should be made on inverter price or rated kW alone. Confirm the exact operating modes, grid approvals, backup output and equipment compatibility.

1. What does a grid-tied solar inverter do?

A grid-tied inverter converts the DC power from the PV array into AC power synchronised with the utility supply. Depending on the approved metering arrangement and local rules, the system can supply household loads and export eligible surplus energy to the grid.

A conventional grid-following inverter normally stops energising its AC output when the utility grid fails. This anti-islanding behaviour is an important safety function: a rooftop system must not unintentionally energise a disconnected utility network.

Important outage point

A standard grid-tied solar installation should not be assumed to provide electricity during a power cut, even when sunlight is available. If backup is required, the system must include equipment specifically designed and approved for that operating mode.

2. What does a hybrid solar inverter do?

A hybrid inverter is designed to coordinate PV generation, the utility supply and battery storage. Depending on the model and installation, it may charge a battery from solar or the grid, supply loads from stored energy, and provide a dedicated backup output when the grid is unavailable.

Backup capability is model-specific. Some systems can supply only a designated essential-load circuit; some have limits on backup power, surge capacity or phase configuration. A hybrid label by itself does not guarantee that the entire home can run during an outage.

4. How to decide for your home

Start with the reason you want solar

  • If your objective is mainly to reduce electricity purchased from the grid, calculate the PV capacity and expected self-consumption first.
  • If your home experiences frequent outages, list the circuits that genuinely need backup rather than assuming every appliance must run.
  • If you need off-grid operation, treat that as a separate design problem involving energy autonomy, battery capacity, seasonal solar yield and a contingency plan.

Prepare an essential-load list

Record each backup appliance, its actual or realistic running power, starting surge where relevant, and the number of hours it must operate. Add the loads that may run simultaneously. This determines the backup inverter power requirement and the usable battery energy required.

For example, a home may choose to back up lights, fans, Wi-Fi and a refrigerator rather than air conditioners, geysers, induction cooking and every socket. The right list depends on household needs and the inverter's continuous and surge limits.

5. Inverter specifications worth checking

  • Rated AC output: Confirm continuous output power, phase arrangement and permitted overload duration.
  • PV input window: Check maximum DC voltage, MPPT operating range, start voltage and number of independent MPPT trackers.
  • PV current limits: Check maximum operating current and short-circuit current per MPPT, including parallel strings.
  • Battery compatibility: Confirm supported battery chemistry, nominal voltage, capacity range, communication protocol and approved battery models.
  • Backup output: Verify whether backup is single-phase or three-phase, its continuous rating, surge capability, changeover behaviour and neutral/earthing requirements.
  • Protection and safety: Review anti-islanding, over/under-voltage and frequency protection, DC isolation, surge protection, earthing and the manufacturer's installation requirements.
  • Monitoring: Check whether the system records PV generation, grid import/export, battery state and load consumption, and whether monitoring needs internet connectivity.
  • Service and warranty: Ask who provides local service, how warranty claims are handled and whether labour, transport and replacement are covered.

6. Battery sizing: do not buy by kWh alone

Battery capacity should be based on the energy needed by the selected backup loads, the required backup duration and the battery manufacturer's permitted operating limits. The usable energy is lower than the nominal battery capacity once depth-of-discharge limits, conversion losses and operating reserves are considered.

First-pass energy estimate

Backup energy (kWh) = Sum of each essential load (kW) × its required operating hours

Then account for inverter efficiency, battery usable depth of discharge, temperature, ageing and any reserve margin. Ask the designer to show these assumptions in the proposal.

Battery power capability is also important. A battery may have enough stored energy but still be unable to supply a high instantaneous load or motor-starting surge. Check both kWh and kW limits.

7. Roof array and inverter must be designed together

Do not select a PV array solely by dividing a target kW figure by panel wattage. The string design must remain within the inverter's DC voltage and current limits across expected operating temperatures. The designer should check open-circuit voltage at the lowest expected module temperature, operating voltage at high temperature, MPPT range and current per tracker.

Also consider shading, roof orientation, module layout, cable routes, isolation, surge protection, earthing, ventilation and access for maintenance. Use the module and inverter datasheets for the exact models proposed.

8. India-specific grid and subsidy checks

Before paying an advance, confirm the current rooftop solar application, approved equipment and interconnection process with your local distribution utility. Metering rules, export compensation, technical limits, fees and documentation can change and may differ by state or consumer category.

If you are considering a residential subsidy, check current eligibility, vendor requirements and approved equipment directly through the official national rooftop solar programme portal and your distribution utility. Do not rely on an old online price list or an installer's verbal assurance.

For Tamil Nadu installations, ask the relevant distribution utility to confirm the applicable process and technical conditions for your service connection. Obtain written details of the proposed metering arrangement and approvals before installation.

9. Questions to ask your solar installer

  • Is the proposal grid-tied, hybrid or off-grid, and what exactly happens during a grid failure?
  • Which circuits are connected to the backup output, and what is the maximum continuous and surge load?
  • What are the PV string voltage and current calculations for the exact module and inverter models?
  • Which battery models are approved by the inverter manufacturer, and what usable energy and discharge power are guaranteed?
  • What protection devices, cable sizes, earthing and isolation arrangements are included?
  • Who handles utility applications, commissioning, monitoring setup and after-sales service?
  • What are the separate equipment, installation, approval and future battery replacement costs?

Frequently asked questions

Can a grid-tied inverter run during a power cut?

A conventional grid-following inverter normally shuts down when the utility supply is lost. Do not assume it has backup capability unless the exact system includes an approved backup function and suitable wiring.

Can a hybrid inverter work without a battery?

Some hybrid models can operate in a grid-connected mode without a battery, but available functions differ by manufacturer and model. Confirm the operating modes in the product manual.

Will a hybrid inverter run my air conditioner during an outage?

Only if the inverter, battery and backup circuit can handle the air conditioner's running demand and starting behaviour, along with other simultaneous loads. Have the actual appliance and system ratings checked.

Is a hybrid inverter always better?

No. If you do not need backup, a suitable grid-tied system may be simpler and avoid the cost and lifecycle considerations of battery storage. Select the architecture that meets your actual requirements.
