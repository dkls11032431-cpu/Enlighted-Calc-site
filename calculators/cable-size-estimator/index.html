Method and assumptions

For DC and single-phase, the loop factor is 2. For balanced three-phase, the factor is √3. The resistive component uses conductor resistivity corrected for temperature: R = ρ₂₀ × [1 + α(T − 20)] × L / S. The AC estimate adds the entered reactance component: ΔV = K × I × L × [R cosφ + X sinφ]. For DC, ΔV = 2 × I × L × R. The required area is solved against the entered allowable voltage drop.

Indicative resistivity constants used: copper 0.01724 Ω·mm²/m with α = 0.00393/°C; aluminium 0.0282 Ω·mm²/m with α = 0.00403/°C. Actual conductor resistance depends on conductor class, stranding, material, operating temperature and AC effects. This simplified model is not a substitute for tabulated cable data.

Before selecting or installing a cable, independently verify: current-carrying capacity (ampacity) for the actual installation method; ambient/soil temperature; grouping and thermal insulation derating; protective-device rating and disconnection time; short-circuit withstand; earth-fault loop impedance; neutral and harmonic loading; terminal temperature limits; mechanical strength; fire performance; and the current applicable Indian standards, utility requirements and manufacturer data. The next standard size shown here is selected for voltage drop only. It is not a safe-cable recommendation.
