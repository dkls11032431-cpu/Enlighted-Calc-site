KNOWLEDGE HUB / ENERGY MANAGEMENT

Energy management: measure, analyse and improve

Practical resources for electricity monitoring, energy audits, maximum-demand management, power factor and submetering. A reliable energy-saving programme starts with accurate measurements and a clear baseline.

01 / BASELINE

Energy audits and consumption baselines

Record energy use over consistent reporting periods and relate it to occupancy, production, weather or operating hours where relevant. Separate measured facts from estimates so that savings can be verified fairly.

02 / DEMAND & POWER FACTOR

Maximum demand and power factor

Understand kW, kVA, kWh, kvar and power factor. Review load profiles and utility billing rules before selecting correction equipment. Capacitor banks require appropriate protection and assessment of harmonics and resonance risks.

03 / SUBMETERING

Energy meters, CTs and monitoring dashboards

Choose meter locations that answer a clear operational question. Document CT ratio, polarity, phase mapping, communication settings and meter multipliers; validate readings against a trusted reference before relying on dashboards.

ENERGY REDUCTION WORKFLOW

A practical sequence for reducing electricity use

  1. Collect bills, meter readings, operating schedules and equipment ratings.
  2. Identify major loads and measure them over representative operating cycles.
  3. Look for avoidable running hours, idle loads, poor controls and maintenance issues.
  4. Prioritise actions by expected savings, cost, safety and operational impact.
  5. Compare post-change consumption against a baseline adjusted for operating conditions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between kW and kVA?

kW is real power. kVA is apparent power. Their relationship depends on power factor for a sinusoidal system; distorted waveforms require additional care.

Why install sub-meters?

Sub-meters help allocate consumption to tenants, departments or equipment and reveal when and where energy is used. Meter accuracy and installation quality are essential.

Does power factor correction always reduce the electricity bill?

Not always. The financial effect depends on the tariff and utility billing rules. A correction system should be selected after analysing the load and power-quality conditions.

Energy recommendations should be verified with site measurements, equipment documentation and applicable electrical standards. Electrical measurements and panel work must be performed by qualified personnel.
